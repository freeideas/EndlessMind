# Explainer video: how Endless Mind works

A script for one video of about five minutes (707 spoken words). It says every word to be spoken and, briefly, what is on screen while it is. Nothing is recorded or rendered until the owner approves the words. The advice it follows is in [VidFact](../../VidFact/specs/making-videos.md) and its [checks](../../VidFact/specs/quality.md).

## The plan

- **Who it is for:** someone who has used an AI coding agent, or heard of one, and has never heard of Endless Mind.
- **The one idea:** describe a game tonight, and the whole world can play it from a link, with no company in the middle.
- **Order:** the result first, then how to make a game, then who you are, then reputation, then the network. Each part answers the question the part before it raises.
- **Pace:** about 2.8 words a second, with something new on screen every 2 to 4 seconds. The times below include short pauses between scenes.
- **The example game:** Lantern Tag, an original game of tag in a dark maze. Each player carries a lantern, and whoever is "it" carries a red one. It is small, clearly for several players, and reads well with the sound off.
- **Sound off:** every scene has a short line of text on screen that carries its point alone.

## Script

### Opening

**1. The result (0:00, 10 s)**

- Show: four screens side by side, each a different player's view of Lantern Tag: small lanterns chasing through a dark maze. The first frame is already the game in motion. Text: "Made last night, by describing it."
- Say: "This game did not exist yesterday. One person described it to an AI. Now their friends are playing it together, from a link."

**2. The name (0:10, 5 s)**

- Show: the four screens pull back into a field of many small lit windows. Title: "Endless Mind".
- Say: "This is Endless Mind: a free, open network for anything AI can make."

### Part 1: make a game

**3. Describe it (0:15, 14 s)**

- Show: a real coding agent in a terminal. The request is typed out: "Make a game called Lantern Tag for Endless Mind. Players carry lanterns in a dark maze, and whoever is 'it' has a red one. Follow github.com/freeideas/EndlessMind." Text: "1. Describe your game."
- Say: "Here is how you make one. Open your AI coding agent: Claude, Codex, Gemini, any of them. Say what you want, and point it at the Endless Mind repository."

**4. What the agent writes (0:29, 14 s)**

- Show: a folder opens into two parts. On one side, several small screens labeled "the display". In the middle, one box labeled "the game server".
- Say: "The agent writes two things. The display: ordinary web pages, one for each player. And the game server: the one part every player connects to, which decides what really happened. You never have to read the code."

**5. Play it (0:43, 8 s)**

- Show: the agent prints a link. A browser opens it, and one lantern walks the maze. Text: "2. Open the link."
- Say: "Then the agent starts the game on your own computer and hands you a link. Open it, and you are playing."

**6. Several players (0:51, 17 s)**

- Show: the game server in the middle. Five small labels arrive one at a time: started, joined, left, message, timer. Arrows carry messages back out to the players' screens, and more lanterns appear.
- Say: "Playing together is built in. The game server hears five things: the game started, a player joined, a player left, a message came in, a timer went off. It answers by sending messages back. That is enough for tag, for chess, for a whole world."

**7. Send it to friends (1:08, 24 s)**

- Show: a house with one computer. Friends in other cities. A go-between appears on the web between them, and lines connect through it. The link is shown, and it stays the same when the game is stopped and started again. Text: "3. Send the link."
- Say: "Your friends are somewhere else, so how do they reach your computer? Through a relay: a go-between on the web that the program finds by itself. It gives your game an address of its own, the same one every time. You rent no server. Your friends install nothing. It works for as long as your computer is on, and we will come back to how a game outlives that."

### Part 2: who you are

**8. No account (1:32, 10 s)**

- Show: a sign-up form with email and password boxes, which is crossed out and falls away. A player marked "Guest" keeps playing.
- Say: "Anyone can play as a guest. To be someone, you need an identity. Here, that is not an account. There is no email and no password."

**9. Your secret phrase (1:42, 14 s)**

- Show: a phone. Twenty-four ordinary words appear in a grid, with a code beside them. A screenshot is taken. Use made-up words for the film, never a phrase anyone uses. Text: "You are 24 words."
- Say: "You are twenty-four ordinary words: your secret phrase. Your phone makes them once, and they never leave your device. Take a screenshot, and keep it to yourself."

**10. Signing in (1:56, 19 s)**

- Show: the game shows a square code. The phone scans it. The phone asks "Sign in the screen in front of you at" and names the site. One tap. The game screen changes from "Guest" to a name.
- Say: "To sign in, the game shows a code. Scan it with your phone, check the site name, and tap once. A small page on your phone, called an EntryPortal, proves to the game that you hold your phrase, without showing the phrase to anyone."

**11. The same player everywhere (2:15, 18 s)**

- Show: the name "Witty Clover" walks from Lantern Tag into three other, different games, and stays the same. Then a phrase catches fire and turns to ash, and a new one appears.
- Say: "The same phrase works in every game on the network, so you are the same player everywhere. Games show you by a friendly name. And if your phrase is ever stolen, you can burn it, which makes it worthless to the thief, and start again."

### Part 3: reputation

**12. A record (2:33, 18 s)**

- Show: the game offers a card: "Finished the Glass Maze in 4 minutes 12 seconds." The phone shows the same words in full. The player approves, and two marks appear on the card: the player's and the game's.
- Say: "When you do something worth remembering, a game offers you a record. Your phone shows you the exact words. If you agree, you add your proof, and the game adds its own."

**13. Anyone can check it (2:51, 11 s)**

- Show: the card is handed to a different game, which checks both marks and lights up. A copy with one word changed is checked and goes dark.
- Say: "Now anyone you show it to can check it, without asking anyone else. Nobody can fake it, and nobody can change a word."

**14. It only grows (3:02, 15 s)**

- Show: a shelf of records. The player keeps most and drops one. No thumbs, no stars. Text: "No likes. No votes. Play."
- Say: "You keep the records you like and drop the rest, so your reputation can only grow. A record shows what you did, not what you own. And there are no likes or votes here. The evidence is play."

**15. Fake crowds count for nothing (3:17, 17 s)**

- Show: a crowd of a thousand identical gray players appears beside a new game, all counting as zero. Then trust spreads as light from one trusted game, to the players who really played there, to the next game they spend time in.
- Say: "Could someone invent a thousand players to cheat? They can invent them, but they count for nothing. A player counts only when a game that is already trusted has their play on record. Trust spreads outward from real play, one step at a time."

### Part 4: the network

**16. No center (3:34, 7 s)**

- Show: a single big server in the middle fades out. What is left is many small points, none bigger than the others.
- Say: "So where does all of this live? Not on our servers. There is no center."

**17. Hosts (3:41, 12 s)**

- Show: one small server lights up. Three labels appear on it: runs games, relays, EntryPortal.
- Say: "Anyone can run a host: one program on a small rented server. A host runs games, relays for games still at home, and offers a spare EntryPortal."

**18. Hosts find each other (3:53, 14 s)**

- Show: a new host knows one other. Cards pass along the lines between hosts until the new one is connected to all of them.
- Say: "Hosts find each other by telling each other. Each one signs a card that says where it is and what it runs, and passes along the cards of every host it knows. Reach one host, and you can find the rest."

**19. Safe to run a stranger's game (4:07, 11 s)**

- Show: a game server inside a sealed glass box on a host. It reaches for the files and the network and cannot touch them. Messages to its players pass through one small slot.
- Say: "A host can run a stranger's game safely, because every game server is sealed. It has no files and no network. All it can do is talk to its players."

**20. Boards and wishes (4:18, 16 s)**

- Show: a board: a list of games with how many players have played each. A player taps "wish" on Lantern Tag. On several hosts the wish count rises, and one host loads the game.
- Say: "Every host has a board: the games the network runs, ranked by real play. See a game you want? Wish for it. Hosts that allow it load the most wished-for games by themselves."

**21. The game outlives your computer (4:34, 9 s)**

- Show: back to the house from scene 7. The computer switches off. Lantern Tag keeps running on three hosts, and the lanterns keep moving.
- Say: "So your game can find a home without you asking anyone. Turn your computer off. The game plays on."

### Close

**22. Your turn (4:43, 12 s)**

- Show: the field of lit windows from scene 2, with new ones still appearing. Text: "endlessmind.com", held for at least four seconds.
- Say: "Endless Mind is not a company's platform. It is a few open rules and one free program. Describe a game tonight, and send someone the link."

## Before filming

The film must show only what really works, on real screens.

- **Lantern Tag has to be real.** Have an agent build it from the request in scene 3, and record that session and the game itself. Scenes 1, 3, 5 and 7 use those recordings, not drawings of them.
- **Scene 3 depends on how the program is installed.** Today that is a checkout of the repository with Deno. If the package is published first ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md), step 1), the scene can show the one-line install instead.
- **Scene 17** is true with the program run from a checkout. `sudo endlessmind setup` has not yet run on a real server (step 2 of the plan), so do not show that command until it has.
- **Scenes 20 and 21** need a second host that loads games by demand, which a host does only when its admin turns that on, and only for wishes from players it has reason to believe. Record them on two real hosts.
- **Scene 9** shows a made-up phrase, and scene 10 a real sign-in recorded with a throwaway identity.
- **Names of AI agents** appear as plain text, without logos.
