# Endless Mind: the problems it solves

> Narration for the explainer. The plan and the reasons are in ../explainer.md. One section per scene; the names match the functions in scenes.js.

## s01 {chapter="I made a game. Now what?"}
You can make a multi-player game with AI in one evening. [#hard] Then comes the hard part. [#here] The game is on your computer, [#friends] and your friends are somewhere else.

## s02
[#name] Endless Mind solves that problem, [#four] and the four that come right after it.

## s03 {chapter="How does anyone else play it?" transition=brush}
The usual answer is to [#a] rent a server, [#b] buy a name, [#c] set up certificates, [#d] and learn to deploy. [#second] That is a second project, and it is not the fun one.

## s04
Instead, [#ask] ask your AI coding agent, [#names] Claude, Codex, Gemini, any of them, [#build] to build the game for Endless Mind. [#point] Just point it at the repository.

## s05
The agent [#writes] writes the game, [#starts] starts it on your own computer, [#link] and hands you a link.

## s06
That link works for [#anyone] anyone, anywhere. [#relay] A relay, a go-between on the web that the program finds by itself, [#carries] carries your friends to your computer. [#rented] You rented nothing. [#install] They install nothing. [#send] Send the link, [#maze] and you are all in the same maze.

## s07
Keeping several players in step is normally the hard part of a game. [#five] Here your agent only has to answer five things: [#e1] the game started, [#e2] a player joined, [#e3] a player left, [#e4] a message came in, [#e5] a timer went off. [#enough] That is enough for tag, for chess, for a whole world.

## s08 {chapter="Keeping who you are" transition=brush}
Second problem. [#keep] Players want to keep what they earn and what makes them unique: [#p1] their progress, [#p2] their name, [#p3] their style. [#every] And they want it in every game, not locked inside one. [#normally] Normally each game builds its own sign-up and passwords, [#behind] and everything a player earned stays behind.

## s09
Here, [#code] your game shows a code. [#scan] The player scans it with their phone [#tap] and taps once. [#in] They are in. [#first] The first time, the phone sets them up in about a minute. [#built] You built nothing.

## s10
It works because the player's identity is [#notyou] not kept by you, [#notus] or by us. [#phone] It lives on their phone, [#phrase] as a secret phrase of twenty-four words [#never] that never leaves it. [#leak] So there is no password to leak, [#lock] and nobody who can lock them out.

## s11
The same identity works in [#every] every game on the network. [#name] The name is theirs to choose: [#rosy] Rosy Nebula can become [#rock] {Rockstar 812|Rockstar eight twelve}, [#everywhere] everywhere at once. [#arrives] So a player arrives in your game with a name and a history, not as a stranger. [#lost] And if the phone is lost, [#shot] a screenshot of the phrase [#back] brings it all back.

## s12 {chapter="A real reputation" transition=brush}
Third problem. [#want] You want your game to earn a real reputation, [#never] so that people who have never heard of it want to play. [#stars] But stars and reviews are easy to fake, [#ai] and AI makes faking them free. [#nobody] So nobody believes them.

## s13
Here, a reputation is [#records] made of records. [#does] When a player does something worth remembering, [#offer] your game offers them a record. [#words] Their phone shows the exact words, [#sign] and if they agree, both sign it. [#check] Anyone can check it. [#fake] Nobody can fake it, [#change] or change a word.

## s14
A player can [#add] add words of their own: [#review] a review that sits on proof that they really played. [#go] Their records go where they go, [#point] and every one of them points back at your game.

## s15
That is your game's reputation: [#real] real play, by real players, [#likes] not likes or votes. [#thousand] A thousand invented players add nothing to it, [#because] because a player counts only when a game that is already trusted [#record] has their play on record. [#bought] It cannot be bought, [#believe] so people believe it.

## s16 {chapter="When your computer is off" transition=brush}
Fourth problem. [#stops] Your game stops when your computer does, [#server] and you still do not want to run a server.

## s17
[#others] Other people do. [#host] Anyone can run a host: the same program, on a small rented server. [#board] Every host has a board of the games the network runs.

## s18
A player who likes your game can [#wish] wish for it there. [#load] Hosts that allow it load the most wished-for games by themselves. [#home] So your game finds a home [#asking] without you asking anyone, or paying anyone.

## s19
[#why] Why would a stranger run your code? [#sealed] Because every game server is sealed. [#files] It has no files [#net] and no network. [#talk] All it can do is talk to its players.

## s20
[#off] Turn your computer off. [#on] The game plays on.

## s21 {chapter="No center" transition=brush}
Last problem. [#platform] Put your game on someone's platform, [#rules] and they can change the rules, [#cut] take a cut, [#switch] or switch it off.

## s22
[#center] Endless Mind has no center. [#cards] Hosts find each other by passing along signed cards that say where they are and what they run. [#reach] Reach one host, and you can find the rest. [#special] No host is special, ours included.

## s23 {chapter="Your turn" transition=brush hold=4.5}
[#describe] So describe a game tonight, [#send] and send someone the link. [#solved] The rest is already solved. [#free] Endless Mind is free, open, and yours to build on.
